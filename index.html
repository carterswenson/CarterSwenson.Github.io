About

Field observations meet atmospheric science.

I am a Ph.D. student at Purdue University studying atmospheric processes through measurements and computational analysis. I am especially interested in connecting observations made in the field with the physical and chemical processes represented in atmospheric models.

My research uses high-time-resolution measurements to investigate the exchange of volatile organic compounds (VOCs) between agricultural landscapes and the atmosphere. By examining how these compounds vary with time, meteorology, and wind direction, I work to better understand their sources and atmospheric behavior.

I enjoy combining instrumentation, programming, and data visualization to turn complex atmospheric datasets into interpretable scientific results.

Research interests

Studying emissions, transport, and atmospheric chemistry.

My work focuses on understanding how atmospheric constituents are emitted, transported, and transformed, particularly over agricultural environments.

01 / FIELD MEASUREMENTS

Atmospheric Chemistry

Measuring volatile organic compounds and investigating how their concentrations and fluxes change across environmental conditions.

02 / ECOSYSTEM EXCHANGE

Surface–Atmosphere Exchange

Using eddy covariance and high-frequency observations to study the exchange of trace gases between land surfaces and the atmosphere.

03 / METEOROLOGY

Boundary-Layer Processes

Exploring how wind, turbulence, temperature, and atmospheric stability influence the transport and distribution of atmospheric compounds.

04 / MODELING

Atmospheric Modeling

Comparing field observations with model estimates to evaluate emissions, transport, and dry-deposition processes.

Current research direction
Investigating VOC exchange over corn and soybean fields in Indiana using PTR-MS, eddy-covariance measurements, and atmospheric model comparisons.
Selected work

From measurements to meaningful results.

A combination of field instrumentation, scientific programming, and model evaluation supports my approach to atmospheric research.

01

VOC Flux Measurements

Processing high-frequency PTR-MS and turbulence data to quantify trace-gas exchange between agricultural fields and the atmosphere.

02

Observed and Modeled Fluxes

Evaluating how atmospheric model estimates compare with measured fluxes, including the influence of emissions and dry deposition.

03

Meteorological Analysis

Investigating wind direction, temperature, and atmospheric conditions to interpret changes in observed concentrations and fluxes.

04

Scientific Computing

Developing reproducible Python and MATLAB workflows for processing, analyzing, and visualizing complex environmental datasets.
